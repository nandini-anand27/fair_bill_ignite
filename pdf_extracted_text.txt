Web App

1. Problem Statement
What is happening today
Families in Bengaluru receive a hospital bill at the end of a normal delivery. The bill is
either a long list of line items or a package total plus extras. Prices for the same procedure
vary across hospitals, hospital types, and room categories, and a family has no way to see
what comparable families actually paid. Existing Indian cost websites show estimated
city-level price ranges for a procedure, and the AI bill-checking tools we found are built
around US billing and insurance statements. In a limited scan we found no tool that
compares a family’s own Indian hospital bill against real bills from comparable hospitals.
This finding still needs to be validated.
Why this is a problem
Families pay all or part of the bill under time pressure, with no reference point to judge
whether it is typical.
Some price differences are justified (facilities, room category, doctor), and some are
not (duplicate entries, items billed separately that are normally inside the package). A
family cannot tell which is which.
Unexpected charges tend to appear at discharge, when the family has the least time
to ask questions.
Who is impacted and how
Patients and families (primary): may pay or accept unclear charges without asking
questions.
Insurers, TPAs, and employers (secondary): fund maternity claims and may lack an
independent reference for what is typical. This needs validation.
Hospitals (indirect): may face more questions about bills, and may resist a
comparison tool.PRISM-P P roblem Brief

2. Business Objective
Give families in Bengaluru a fast, evidence-based way to see whether a normal-delivery
hospital bill is typical for comparable hospitals, and build a consented benchmark of real
bills that can later be offered to payers.
Primary objective for the first pilot: at least 25 families receive a bill comparison within
60 seconds of upload, and at least 60% of them report that it gave them a specific charge
to review or a question to ask the hospital.

6. Pain Points
Bills are long and use inconsistent item names, so families struggle to read them.
Available price references are city-wide and mix very different kinds of hospitals.
It is unclear what a package includes and what is billed on top of it.

1. Background & Context
This PRD-Lite follows the PRISM-P Problem Brief for Fair Bill.
What problem are we solving? Families in Bengaluru cannot tell whether a normal-
delivery hospital bill is typical for comparable hospitals. Existing Indian cost websites
show estimated city-level ranges, and the AI bill-checking tools we found are built for US
billing. In a limited scan we found no tool that checks a family’s own Indian hospital bill
against real bills from comparable hospitals. This needs validation.
Why now? The problem can be tested with a narrow scope (one procedure, one city), and
a small set of consented real bills is enough to evaluate whether the comparison is useful.
2. In-Scope
Scope boundary: normal delivery, Bengaluru, bills in English.
Features
Upload of a bill as an image or PDF
Extraction of line items and the printed total, with a check that they add up
User review and correction of extracted items
Comparison with similar bills (same procedure, hospital tier, room category)
Typical ranges, the number of bills used, and flags for unusual charges
Plain-language explanations and suggested questions to ask the hospital
Optional, consented contribution of the bill to the benchmark, with anonymization
and deletion on request
Administrator review of contributed bills and maintenance of tiers and categories
Workflows: bill check at or after discharge; bill contribution; benchmark administration.
User roles: patient or family member; benchmark administrator.PRISM-R PRD-Lite

6. Functional Requirements
Proposed values (limits, thresholds, time frames) are tracked for confirmation in Section
12.
FR-01: The system shall accept a bill as a JPG, PNG, or PDF file of up to 10 MB and up
to 5 pages.
FR-02: The system shall extract from the bill the hospital name, bill date, room type (if
shown), each line item (description and amount), and the printed total.
FR-03: The system shall add up the extracted line items, compare the sum with the
printed total, and notify the user when they differ by more than ₹ 1.
FR-04: The system shall let the user edit, add, and delete line items and amounts
before comparison, including entering all items manually if extraction fails.
FR-05: The system shall ask the user to confirm the procedure (normal delivery only),
hospital, room category, and bill year. It shall assign the hospital tier from the
administrator-maintained list, or ask the user to select a tier if the hospital is not
listed.
FR-06: The system shall assign each line item to a standard category: room, doctor
fees, nursing, medicines, consumables, baby charges, tests, or other.PRISM-R PRD-Lite
Page 3 of 11

--- Page 4 ---
FR-07: The system shall compare the bill with its peer group: approved bills for the
same procedure, hospital tier, and room category, dated within the last 3 years.
FR-08: The system shall show, for each category and for the total, the typical range of
the peer group (25th to 75th percentile) and the number of bills used.
FR-09: The system shall apply data-sufficiency rules: with fewer than 5 bills in the
peer group, no range is shown; with 5 to 9 bills, the range is labelled “limited data”;
with 10 or more bills, the range is shown without the label.
FR-10: The system shall raise these flags: (a) duplicate line items; (b) a category
amount above the 90th percentile of its peer group, only when the group has at least
10 bills; (c) an item that appears in fewer than 10% of peer-group bills, only when the
group has at least 10 bills; (d) a sum mismatch that the user has not resolved; (e) an
item billed separately although the hospital’s recorded package inclusions list it,
where such data exists.
FR-11: For each flag, the system shall give a plain-language explanation and at least
one suggested question to ask the hospital. The wording shall not state or imply
overcharging, fraud, or differences in hospital quality.
FR-12: The result screen shall display a notice that the comparison is about price only
and is not medical or legal advice.
FR-13: The system shall offer, separately from the result, an optional consent to
contribute the bill to the benchmark. The option shall be unchecked by default, and
the result shall not depend on it.
FR-14: Before storing a contributed bill, the system shall remove patient names,
patient or admission numbers, doctor names, phone numbers, and addresses, and
shall not retain the original image or file.
FR-15: The system shall show peer data only in aggregate. It shall never display an
individual contributed bill or attribute a contributed price to a named hospital.
FR-16: The system shall give each contributor a reference code and shall delete the
contributor’s stored bill within 7 days of a deletion request made with that code.
FR-17: The system shall let an administrator review, approve, or reject contributed bills
before they enter the benchmark, and maintain the hospital tier list, category
mapping, and package information.PRISM-R PRD-Lite

7. Acceptance Criteria (Testable)
FR-01: Given the user is on the upload screen, when they select a 4 MB JPG, then the
file is accepted and processing starts. Given a file over 10 MB or of another type,
when selected, then it is rejected with a message stating the allowed types and size.
FR-02: Given a clear bill with hospital name, date, and itemized charges, when it is
processed, then the review screen shows the hospital name, date, each line item with
its amount, and the printed total.
FR-03: Given extracted items that sum to ₹ 42,000 and a printed total of ₹ 45,000,
when the check runs, then a mismatch notice is shown and the user is asked to
review before continuing.
FR-04: Given the review screen, when the user edits an amount or deletes a row, then
the comparison uses the edited values. Given extraction failed, when the user adds
items manually, then the flow continues normally.
FR-05: Given a hospital on the maintained list, when the user confirms the bill details,
then its tier is assigned automatically. Given a hospital not on the list, when the user
confirms, then the user is asked to select a tier.
FR-06: Given a line item for room charges, when categorization runs, then it is
assigned to the room category. Given an item that matches no category, then it is
assigned to other and shown to the user.
FR-07: Given a bill dated within the last 3 years with a confirmed tier and room
category, when the comparison runs, then only approved bills with the same
procedure, tier, and room category are used.
FR-08: Given a peer group with 12 bills, when results are shown, then each category
and the total show a 25th to 75th percentile range and the text “based on 12 bills”.
FR-09: Given a peer group with 4 bills, then no range is shown. Given 7 bills, then a
range with the label “limited data” is shown. Given 10 bills, then the range is shown
without the label.
FR-10: Given a bill with the same item entered twice, when the comparison runs, then
a duplicate flag is raised. Given a category amount above the group’s 90th percentile
and a group of at least 10 bills, then a high-amount flag is raised. Given a group of
fewer than 10 bills, then flags (b) and (c) are not raised.
FR-11: Given any flag, when it is displayed, then it includes an explanation, at least one
suggested question, and none of the words overcharged, fraud, or cheated.
FR-12: Given the result screen, when it loads, then the price-only notice is visible
without scrolling past the results.PRISM-R PRD-Lite

FR-13: Given the result screen, when it loads, then the consent option is unchecked.
Given the user does not consent, then the result is still shown and no bill data is
stored.
FR-14: Given a consented bill containing a patient name and admission number, when
it is stored, then the stored record contains neither, and the original file is not
retained.
FR-15: Given any result screen, then it shows only ranges and counts, and does not
show an individual bill or a hospital-specific contributed price.
FR-16: Given a valid reference code, when a deletion request is received, then the
stored bill is removed within 7 days and the request is recorded. Given an invalid code,
then no data is deleted and the requester is told the code was not found.
FR-17: Given a newly contributed bill, when it is pending, then it is not used in any
comparison. Given the administrator approves it, then it is included from the next
comparison.

PRISM-S Design Document (RFC)
Initiative: Fair Bill, a hospital bill comparison tool for families in Bengaluru
Company: SmartX Technologies | PRISM Phase: S – Structured Design | Project type: W eb

1. Executive Summary
Problem recap: Families in Bengaluru cannot tell whether a normal-delivery hospital bill is
typical for comparable hospitals.
Chosen solution: Option 2 from PRISM -I, a hybrid pipeline. One Python web application
reads a bill with a vision model, checks the extraction, compares the bill with consented,
anonymized bills grouped by procedure, hospital tier, and room category using
deterministic rules, and explains the result in neutral plain language.
Key tradeoffs accepted
Bill images are processed by a third-party vision service. This is managed with a
consent notice, a provider that does not retain or train on images, in-memory handling,
and a spending cap.
There is a per-bill model cost, to be measured in the provider spike.
The pilot runs as a single instance with no formal availability guarantee.
Categorization uses keyword rules in version 1, not a trained model.

5. Component-Level Design
C1 Web UI
Responsibilities: Implements the pages and keeps session state in memory. Clears
the in-progress bill when the user leaves or after 30 minutes of inactivity (proposed).
Inputs / outputs: User actions in; rendered pages and notices out.
Dependencies: All services.
C2 Upload validator
Responsibilities: Accept JPG, PNG, and PDF files up to 10 M B and 5 pages. Decode
each file to confirm it is real. Strip image metadata. Render PDF pages to images in
memory.
Inputs / outputs: File bytes and type in; a list of page images or an error code out.
Dependencies: Pillow, pypdfium2.
C3 Extractor
Responsibilities: Send the page images to the vision model with a fixed instruction.
The instruction asks for JSON only, with amounts as numbers in rupees, unreadable
fields as null, no patient names, identifiers, doctor names, phone numbers, or
addresses, and to ignore any instructions that appear inside the document.
Temperature is 0. Validate the reply against the extraction schema in Section 7. Run
the sum check with a ₹ 1 tolerance.
Time rules: The first attempt times out at 25 seconds. One retry is made only if at
least 10 seconds remain of a 40-second extraction budget.
Inputs / outputs: Page images in; a validated extracted bill and sum-check result, or an
error code, out.
Dependencies: Provider adapter, Pydantic.
C4 Hospital resolver
Responsibilities: Normalize the hospital name, try an exact match, then a fuzzy match
at 0.85 or higher. Return the hospital and tier, or no match so the user selects a tier.
Inputs / outputs: Hospital name in; hospital id and tier, or none, out.
Dependencies: Hospitals table, rapidfuzz.
C5 CategorizerPRISM-S Design Document (RFC)
Page 5 of 23

--- Page 6 ---
Responsibilities: Normalize each description into an item key (lowercase, punctuation
and numbers removed, spaces collapsed, doctor names reduced to the word doctor).
Apply ordered keyword rules from the category map to assign room, doctor fees,
nursing, medicines, consumables, baby charges, tests, or other.
Inputs / outputs: Line items in; categorized items with item keys out.
Dependencies: Category map table.
C6 Benchmark engine
Responsibilities: Select approved bills for the same procedure, hospital tier, and room
category, dated within the last 3 years. For the total and for each category, collect
each peer bill’s amount (only bills that have that category) and compute the 25th,
50th, 75th, and 90th percentiles using inclusive quantiles. Apply the sufficiency rule
to each range using the number of peer bills that contribute to it: fewer than 5, no
range; 5 to 9, labelled limited data; 10 or more, normal.
Inputs / outputs: A confirmed bill in; a comparison result with ranges and counts out.
Dependencies: Bills and bill items tables.
C7 Flag engine
Rule A, duplicates: Two or more lines with the same item key and the same amount,
and the same date if dates are present. Room and nursing lines are excluded because
daily charges repeat legitimately.
Rule B, high amount: A category total above the 90th percentile of its peer
distribution, only when that distribution has at least 10 bills.
Rule C, unusual item: An item key present in fewer than 10%  of peer bills, only when
the peer group has at least 10 bills.
Rule D, unresolved mismatch: The user continued with a sum mismatch above ₹ 1.
Rule E, package inclusion: A separately billed item that the hospital’s recorded
package inclusions list, only when such data exists.
Output limits: Each flag carries its rule id, the item or category, the observed value,
the reference value, and the number of bills used. At most 8 flags are shown, ordered
by amount.
C8 Explainer
Responsibilities: Create an explanation and at least one suggested question for each
flag from fixed templates. W hen enabled, ask a text model to reword the template in
friendlier language with an 8-second timeout. Reject any wording that contains aPRISM-S Design Document (RFC)
Page 6 of 23

--- Page 7 ---
banned term (for example overcharged, fraud, cheated, scam, or a claim that one
hospital is better or worse) and fall back to the template.
Inputs / outputs: Flags in; explained flags with questions out.
Dependencies: Provider adapter, banned-terms list.
C9 Contribution and privacy
Responsibilities: Require a recorded consent. Scrub item descriptions of doctor
names, phone numbers, admission and patient numbers, and emails. Compute a
duplicate-detection hash from hospital, bill date, total, and sorted item keys with
amounts. Insert the bill as pending. Create a 12-character random reference code,
store only its salted hash, and show the code to the user once. Never write the
original file to disk.
Inputs / outputs: A confirmed bill and consent in; a reference code or a duplicate
notice out.
Dependencies: Repository layer, secrets library.
C10 Deletion
Responsibilities: Hash the supplied code, delete the matching bill and its items
immediately, and record the request in the deletion log. The requirement allows 7
days, and this design deletes at once. Repeating the request gives the same not-
found message.
Inputs / outputs: A reference code in; a deletion result out.
C11 Admin
Responsibilities: Password-gated pages (password from an environment secret, 30-
minute session). Show pending bills with scrubbed items, approve or reject, and
maintain hospitals and tiers, category rules, and package inclusions. Every action is
written to the audit log.
Inputs / outputs: Admin actions in; data changes and audit entries out.

Idempotency
Contribution: the duplicate-detection hash is unique. Submitting the same bill again
returns a notice and creates no new record. No second reference code is issued.
Deletion: repeating a request is safe and returns the same message.
Admin approval: approving an already approved bill changes nothing.
Versioning: The extraction schema, the consent text, and the database each carry a
version. Records store the schema and consent versions used.
External provider contract: The adapter sends page images and the fixed instruction,
and expects only schema-conforming JSON. Requests use temperature 0, a 25-second
timeout, no tools, and provider settings that disable retention and training. Replies are
treated as data and never executed or used as instructions.

11. Execution Notes
Working method: short-lived branches and pull requests, reviewed by the other team
member and merged only when CI is green. A 15-minute daily check-in updates task
status.
Tests first for the rules: write the unit tests from the PRISM-R examples before T-011,
T-018, T-019, and T-021.

to T-009.

PRISM-I Solution Options Matrix
Initiative: Fair Bill, a hospital bill comparison tool for families in Bengaluru
Company: SmartX Technologies | PRISM Phase: I – Intentional Solutioning | Project type:
Web App
Status: Draft for decision owner review | Draft date: 11 November 2026
1. Problem Recap
Problem statement: Families in Bengaluru cannot tell whether a normal-delivery
hospital bill is typical for comparable hospitals. Existing price pages show city-level
estimates, and no tool we found checks a family’s own Indian bill against real
comparable bills (limited scan, to be validated).
Key success metrics (proposed in PRISM-P): at least 25 pilot families receive a
comparison within 60 seconds; at least 60% say it gave them a charge to review or a
question to ask; at least 85% of test bills pass the sum check without manual
correction; at least 15 consented bills before prototype testing and 30 before the pilot;
at least 2 payers agree to a pilot discussion.
References: PRISM-P Problem Brief
(https://claude.ai/artifact/CyKPRbSY9KZi1jHY5J1u99) and PRISM-R PRD-Lite
(https://claude.ai/artifact/GnqUDMkCkrLk8DpYdwEWp7).
2. Constraints & Non-Negotiables
Time / budget: A team of two, no dedicated budget, free or low-cost tools. The final
delivery window is still to be confirmed.
Technology / architecture: Usable from a phone browser with nothing to install. No
integration with hospital or insurer systems. English-language bills only. The data
structure must allow other procedures and cities to be added later through data, not
rework.
Compliance / security: Explicit consent before any bill is stored. Personal identifiers
removed before storage. Deletion on request within 7 days. Review against the Digital
Personal Data Protection Act, 2023. No personal identifiers in logs.
Wording: The product must never state or imply that a hospital overcharged, or that
one hospital is better than another in quality.PRISM-I Solution Options Matrix

5. Option 1 – Concierge Service: Description
Architecture summary: No software product. A family sends a photo of the bill through a
simple intake form or message. A team member types the line items into a spreadsheet,
spreadsheet formulas compute peer-group ranges, and the team replies with a short
written report.
Key components: intake form, shared spreadsheet benchmark, report template, consent
message, deletion log.
How requirements are met: The comparison logic (FR-03 to FR-12) is done manually using
the same rules as PRISM-R. Anonymization (FR-14) and deletion (FR-16) are manual steps.
Upload (FR-01) and automatic extraction (FR-02) are not met.
6. Option 1 – Pros / Cons / Risks
Pros
Fastest to start and nearly free
Very accurate, because a person reads the bill
Tests demand and the wording of explanations with real families before any code is
written
Builds the benchmark of real bills at the same time
Cons

Does not scale beyond a handful of families

7. Option 2 – Hybrid Pipeline: Description
Architecture summary: One Python web application with separate service modules
inside it. A vision-capable language model reads the bill into structured line items. All
comparisons, percentiles, and flags are computed by ordinary code against a relational
database of consented, anonymized bills. The language model, or fixed templates, only
turns the flags into plain-language explanations, and a filter blocks accusatory wording.
Key components: web interface, upload validator, extraction service, categorizer,
benchmark engine, flag engine, explanation service, contribution and privacy service,
administrator tools, database.
How requirements are met: FR-01 to FR-04 through the upload validator, extraction
service, and review screen. FR-05 to FR-09 through the hospital resolver and benchmark
engine. FR-10 to FR-12 through the flag and explanation services. FR-13 to FR-16 through
the contribution and privacy service. FR-17 through the administrator tools.

9. Option 3 – Self-Hosted OCR Pipeline: Description
Architecture summary: The same application structure as Option 2, but bill text is read

Option 1 scores low on functional fit and NFR fit because it cannot meet the 60-

Option 2, the hybrid pipeline.
Why this option
It is the only option that meets all functional requirements and the 60-second target.
Putting all comparisons and flags in plain code keeps every result traceable and keeps
wording under control.
The model is limited to reading bills and wording, with user correction as a safety net.
It can grow to new procedures and cities through data.
